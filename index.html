2. Scan Type

Select one primary scan type. Some scans require elevated privileges in real environments; this tool explains them conceptually.

3. Ports

Examples: -p 22, -p 22,80,443, -p 1-1000, -p-, --top-ports 100

4. Service & Version Detection

-sV probes services to identify product and version. Intensity controls probe aggressiveness (0 light, 9 thorough). --version-all is equivalent to intensity 9.

5. OS Detection

OS fingerprinting compares network responses to known signatures; accuracy depends on open/closed ports and host behavior.

6. Timing

Trade-offs: slower templates reduce network impact and can evade simple rate-based detection; faster templates increase speed but may reduce reliability or disrupt fragile services.

7. Output Options

8. NSE Script Scanning Advanced

Authorization required: Intrusive, exploit-oriented, and authentication-testing scripts must only be used with explicit permission in carefully isolated labs. vuln scripts can be disruptive.

9. Firewall & Evasion Concepts Advanced

These options are presented for defensive understanding and authorized lab analysis. Do not use them to bypass security controls on unauthorized networks.

Fragmentation/MTU split probes; decoys obscure origin in logs; source-port selection may interact with firewall rules. Timing also influences detectability. Understand these to design and monitor defenses.
